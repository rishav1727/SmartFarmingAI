STRAWBERRY DISEASE CROP MANAGEMENT GUIDE

1. Strawberry Leaf Scorch (Diplocarpon earlianum)
Symptoms: Small, irregular, dark purple to red spots on upper leaf surfaces. Spots enlarge and centers turn tan to brown while margins remain purple-red, giving a scorched appearance. Severe infections cause early defoliation.
Treatment and Cure:
- Chemical Control: Apply Captan 50% WP at 3g/L. Myclobutanil 10% WP at 1g/L. Azoxystrobin at 1ml/L as systemic control. Spray every 10-14 days from early spring through harvest.
- Biological Control: Bacillus subtilis (Serenade) at 5ml/L foliar spray. Neem oil (5ml/L) with soap emulsifier every 7 days.
- Preventative Control: Remove and destroy infected leaves after harvest. Avoid overhead irrigation. Plant resistant strawberry varieties. Ensure adequate plant spacing (30cm x 40cm) for good air circulation.
- Fungicide: Captan, Myclobutanil, Azoxystrobin, Tebuconazole

2. Strawberry Gray Mold (Botrytis cinerea)
Symptoms: Brown water-soaked lesions on berries, flowers, and leaves. Gray fluffy mold sporulation on infected tissue in humid conditions. Infected berries rot rapidly.
Treatment and Cure:
- Chemical Control: Iprodione 50% WP at 1g/L. Fenhexamid (Teldor) at 1g/L. Pyrimethanil at 1ml/L. Begin spray program at early bloom and repeat every 7-10 days.
- Biological Control: Trichoderma harzianum 10g/L foliar spray. Bacillus subtilis preventive spray.
- Preventative Control: Harvest ripe fruit promptly. Remove decaying berries. Improve air circulation through mulching and proper spacing.

3. Strawberry Angular Leaf Spot (Xanthomonas fragariae)
Symptoms: Small water-soaked irregular lesions on lower leaf surface that turn brown and angular. White bacterial exudate may dry to a white crust on leaf surfaces.
Treatment and Cure:
- Chemical Control: Copper Oxychloride 50% WP at 3g/L. Copper Hydroxide bactericide spray every 7-10 days during wet weather.
- Preventative Control: Avoid overhead irrigation. Use certified disease-free transplants. Rotate planting sites every 3 years.
